# Plastic-waste project feasibility gate checklist

This checklist is designed to prevent a spreadsheet case from being mistaken for a bankable or permitted project. A project should not progress to investment claims merely because its base-case NPV is positive.

## Gate 1 — Feedstock security

- [ ] Identify polymer types and target waste streams.
- [ ] Complete representative sampling across seasons and suppliers.
- [ ] Measure contamination, moisture, dirt, multilayer fractions and reject rate.
- [ ] Estimate collection radius, transport requirements and competing uses.
- [ ] Confirm annual quantity, variability and quality with written supplier evidence.
- [ ] Check whether feedstock is already committed to other recyclers or aggregators.

**Evidence to retain:** sampling protocol, lab results, supplier agreements, collection maps and volume history.

## Gate 2 — Process and material balance

- [ ] Define the exact processing route (mechanical recycling, sorting/washing, chemical conversion or another route).
- [ ] Obtain independent engineering review of the process design.
- [ ] Validate yields with representative pilot or operational data.
- [ ] Show all material streams: saleable products, rejects, wastewater, emissions, moisture loss and residues.
- [ ] Specify product quality and test methods.
- [ ] Check energy, water, chemical and consumable requirements.
- [ ] Identify shutdown, cleaning, maintenance and downtime assumptions.

**Evidence to retain:** process flow diagram, mass/energy balance, pilot report, equipment datasheets and product specifications.

## Gate 3 — Market and revenue

- [ ] Identify buyers for every product stream.
- [ ] Obtain written off-take terms or credible market quotations.
- [ ] Verify price, quality grade, delivery conditions, volume limits and rejection rules.
- [ ] Distinguish gross product mass from saleable product meeting buyer specification.
- [ ] Confirm whether a gate fee is legally and commercially collectable.
- [ ] Stress-test price volatility and buyer concentration.

**Evidence to retain:** buyer correspondence, purchase/offtake contracts, quality requirements and dated price sources.

## Gate 4 — Engineering, environment and safety

- [ ] Screen location, land and utility requirements.
- [ ] Review air emissions, wastewater, process residues and noise.
- [ ] Assess fire, explosion, chemical, thermal and mechanical hazards relevant to the selected process.
- [ ] Define worker protection, emergency response and maintenance safety.
- [ ] Confirm environmental clearances, permits and operating approvals with competent authorities.
- [ ] Assess impacts on nearby communities, traffic and local services.
- [ ] Determine residue classification and compliant disposal route.

**Evidence to retain:** environmental assessment, hazard study, permits, monitoring plan and waste-management contracts.

## Gate 5 — Financial model quality

- [ ] Separate CAPEX, fixed OPEX, variable OPEX, working capital and financing.
- [ ] Obtain dated equipment, civil, installation, utility and contingency estimates.
- [ ] Model realistic capacity ramp-up and plant uptime.
- [ ] Include maintenance and major replacement costs.
- [ ] Use consistent nominal/real currency and discount-rate conventions.
- [ ] Include taxes, depreciation, debt and working capital where relevant.
- [ ] Test downside cases for lower yield, lower prices, higher feedstock/transport cost, lower uptime and CAPEX overrun.
- [ ] Review NPV, IRR, payback and cash-flow profile together.
- [ ] Confirm there is no double-counting of revenue, gate fees or residual credits.

**Evidence to retain:** model assumptions register, quotation log, model version, scenario outputs and independent review.

## Gate 6 — Decision

- [ ] Technical feasibility supported by engineering evidence.
- [ ] Feedstock and offtake secured on acceptable terms.
- [ ] Legal and environmental pathways confirmed.
- [ ] Health, safety and community risks adequately controlled.
- [ ] Financial downside remains acceptable to the intended investor or sponsor.
- [ ] Unresolved assumptions are disclosed.

A no-go or redesign decision is a valid output. The purpose of the model is to structure an evidence-based decision, not to guarantee a positive investment result.
